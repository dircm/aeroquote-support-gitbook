# Circuit sessions

FlightAware often opens a new flight for every circuit. A helicopter lesson or a fixed-wing touch-and-go detail can be six hops, or twenty, a few minutes apart. AeroQuote keeps those as **one recorded flight** when they are the same aircraft returning to the same airport.

## The 10 minute rule

A later hop joins the open session when it takes off **less than 10 minutes** after the previous landing.

| First hop | Next takeoff | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 02:34–02:45 | 02:54 | One flight. The gap is 9 minutes. |
| 02:34–02:45 | 02:59 | A new flight. The gap is 14 minutes. |

A gap of exactly 10 minutes also starts a new flight.

The session uses the **first takeoff** and the **last landing**. The minutes shown are the **sum of the airborne time** on each hop, not the time spent on the ground between them. The row is labelled **No track recorded**, and the list shows the circuit count.

No map is drawn for these flights. Each hop is only a short loop at the field, and stitching them into one line is a poor picture of the lesson.

## Turning a session into a training flight

On **Recorded flights**, open the session and choose **Training**. That creates one training flight from it.

Hops that stayed separate, because the gap was 10 minutes or more, can still be ticked together and converted with **Training**. They become one training flight.
